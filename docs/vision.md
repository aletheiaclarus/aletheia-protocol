# The Clarus Protocol — Vision

## Purpose

Clarus is an open proposal for making public institutions more transparent, auditable, and better managed.

Its purpose is not to attack governments or replace democratic institutions.

Its purpose is to make public information easier to verify.

## The Core Idea

Public institutions manage resources that belong to the public.

Citizens should therefore be able to independently verify:

- where public money comes from
- where it goes
- what it buys
- what was promised
- what was delivered
- how public resources are managed
- how democratic processes are conducted

The guiding principle is:

> **Verify, do not trust.**

This does not mean assuming that people or institutions are dishonest.

It means designing systems so that important claims can be checked independently.

## What Clarus Provides

Clarus is intended to operate as a public verification layer connecting information from existing institutions and official sources.

It can organize and make verifiable:

1. **Public finances**
   - revenue
   - expenditure
   - debt
   - assets and liabilities
   - transfers
   - public projects

2. **Public contracts**
   - tenders
   - suppliers
   - contract values
   - modifications
   - payments
   - delivery and completion

3. **Public compensation**
   - salaries
   - allowances
   - benefits
   - compensation rules

4. **Government promises and performance**
   - what was promised
   - when it was promised
   - whether it changed
   - whether it was delivered
   - supporting evidence

5. **Democratic processes**
   - voter eligibility
   - anonymous voting
   - independent verification
   - auditable results

## Transparency With Privacy

Clarus follows a simple distinction:

> **Public information should be open. Personal information should be protected.**

The system should minimize the collection of personal data.

Sensitive information should not be placed on a public ledger simply because transparency is desirable.

Transparency must not become surveillance.

## Evidence, Not Accusations

Automated systems may identify unusual patterns or potential risks.

Examples include:

- unusually high prices
- repeated suppliers
- contracts divided into smaller amounts
- unusually short tender periods
- payments made before expected delivery
- unexpected contract modifications

These signals are **not proof of wrongdoing**.

A Clarus signal should identify:

- the rule that generated the signal
- the underlying data
- the source
- the relevant time period
- the level of uncertainty
- available explanations or responses

The purpose is to make investigation easier, not to replace investigation.

## Technology

Clarus may use cryptography, distributed systems, digital signatures, hashes, and blockchain technology where these provide useful guarantees.

Technology does not make information automatically true.

A blockchain can help demonstrate that a record was not silently changed after publication.

It cannot prove that the original information was correct.

Therefore, independent source verification remains essential.

## Independence

Clarus should not be controlled by a government, political party, individual politician, private company, or single organization.

Its governance should favor:

- open standards
- open-source software
- independent audits
- transparent funding
- reproducible data
- public documentation
- multiple independent participants

## Global Protocol, Local Implementation

Clarus is intended as a common protocol rather than a single national system.

The principles should remain consistent across countries.

Implementation should respect each country's:

- constitution
- laws
- institutions
- privacy requirements
- electoral system
- public-data infrastructure

The first implementation can be developed for Spain, with other countries able to build compatible implementations.

## Long-Term Goal

The long-term goal is simple:

> **Make public institutions easier to understand, easier to verify, and easier to hold accountable through evidence.**

Clarus does not seek to decide what citizens should believe.

It seeks to give citizens better information with which to make their own decisions.
